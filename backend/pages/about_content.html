iLit: immigration litigation intelligence

iLit is a website for researching Canadian immigration case law. It brings public court and tribunal decisions together in one place, lets you search them, and shows each one in a clean, easy-to-read page. It works in an ordinary web browser, with nothing to install.

–court and tribunal decisions in the library
–Federal Court immigration cases tracked on the Court’s public record
–activity records on those cases, such as filings, hearings and rulings

Who it is for

iLit is designed for the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) when it deals with immigration litigation, and above all for its Hearings and Litigation Division. It is a design goal, not an endorsement: the Agency has not adopted or approved it.

Anyone who reads immigration decisions for work or study, such as lawyers, researchers and students, can use it the same way.

Why it was made

Immigration litigation keeps growing. More cases are filed and decided every year, and the people who handle them have more to keep up with.

A great deal of useful information about those cases is already public, but it is spread over several websites and hard to use together. I built iLit to gather that public information in one place and connect it, so that a question like “how has the Court treated this issue?” can be answered in minutes instead of hours.

What you can do with it

Find decisions

Search by case name, citation or a few words, and narrow the results by court, year or type of case.

 Read them clearly

Open a decision in a clean page with numbered paragraphs. The cases and laws it mentions are marked, and you can jump straight to them.

 See the bigger picture

Look across many decisions at once: a judge’s past decisions, which cases are relied on most, and how Federal Court cases move from filing to the end.

 Keep and check your work

Pin the decisions you need in the Workbench, and check the cases and laws cited in a document of your own. Your document is read and then forgotten; it is never kept.

Where the information comes from

Everything in iLit comes from public sources: decisions of the Federal Court, the Federal Court of Appeal, the Supreme Court of Canada and the Immigration and Refugee Board, and the Federal Court’s public record of activity on each case. Every decision links back to its original, so you can always check it.

iLit is a research aid. It does not give legal advice and it does not predict results.
