About · Overview

iLit: immigration litigation intelligence

iLit is a website for researching Canadian immigration case law. It brings public court and tribunal decisions together in one place, lets you search them, and shows each one in a clean, easy-to-read page. It works in an ordinary web browser, with nothing to install.

–court and tribunal decisions in the library
–Federal Court immigration cases tracked on the Court’s public record
–activity records on those cases, such as filings, hearings and rulings

These numbers are counted fresh when this page opens. A dash means the count could not be read just now.

At a glance

What

A website to search and read Canadian immigration decisions, with the cases and laws they rely on.

Who

Built for CBSA’s Hearings and Litigation Division; open to anyone who reads these decisions.

Why

Litigation keeps growing, and the public information about it is scattered and hard to use.

How

Public decisions are gathered into one database and read by fixed rules. No AI is used when you search.

Who it is for

iLit is designed for the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) when it deals with immigration litigation, and above all for its Hearings and Litigation Division. It is a design goal, not an endorsement: the Agency has not adopted or approved it.

Anyone who reads immigration decisions for work or study, such as lawyers, researchers and students, can use it the same way.

Why it was made

Immigration litigation keeps growing. More cases are filed and decided every year, and the people who handle them have more to keep up with.

A great deal of useful information about those cases is already public, but it is spread over several websites and hard to use together. I built iLit to gather that public information in one place and connect it, so that a question like “how has the Court treated this issue?” can be answered in minutes instead of hours.

What it does, and how it compares

The Division researches with CanLII today, a free and very good service. iLit is built to sit beside it, not to replace it, and to add what it does not do for immigration litigation.

Find and read decisions

Search by name, citation or a few words, then read each decision in a clean page with numbered paragraphs and every cited case and law marked.

Why it helps

You get to the passage that matters quickly, and can follow a citation straight to the paragraph it points to.

What exists today

CanLII also searches full text and notes up cited paragraphs. iLit adds filters for immigration work, such as outcome and type of case.
See who won, by issue

Each decision carries its outcome, for the Minister or the applicant, worked out by fixed rules and always shown with the number of decisions behind it.

Why it helps

It shows how the Court has actually ruled on an issue, not just what the cases say. Outcomes are still being checked against hand-reviewed decisions.

What exists today

I found no outcome coding for Federal Court immigration decisions in CanLII, Westlaw or Lexis.
Every Federal Court immigration case

The Court’s public record for each immigration case, turned into clear steps from filing to leave, hearing and judgment.

Why it helps

Most cases end without a published decision. Leave rates, timelines and workload can only be seen from the full record.

What exists today

The Court’s website looks up one file at a time and publishes overall totals each quarter.
Which authorities matter

For any case: who cites it, how that has changed over time, and how the citing decisions turned out.

Why it helps

It shows which authorities carry the day in immigration cases, and which are fading.

What exists today

CanLII lists citing cases and ranks them by discussion, without their outcomes. Paid tools flag later treatment; the Division does not hold them.
Check a document’s citations

Drop in a memo and see every case and law it cites, linked to the library. Your document is read and then forgotten; it is never kept.

Why it helps

A quick check that every authority is found and points where it should, before a document goes out.

What exists today

CanLII has no such check. Citation checkers come with paid services the Division does not hold.

Comparisons are from public product information checked in October 2026. CanLII covers far more courts and tribunals than iLit does.

Where the information comes from

Everything in iLit comes from public sources: decisions of the Federal Court, the Federal Court of Appeal, the Supreme Court of Canada and the Immigration and Refugee Board, and the Federal Court’s public record of activity on each case. Every decision links back to its original, so you can always check it.

iLit is a research aid. It does not give legal advice and it does not predict results.
