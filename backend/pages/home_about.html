About

A research website for Canadian immigration case law

iLit brings public court and tribunal decisions together in one place, lets you search them, and shows each one in a clean, easy-to-read page. It works in an ordinary web browser, with nothing to install.

What it is

A website to search and read Canadian immigration decisions. Public decisions are read once by fixed rules and stored, so no AI is used when you search.

Who it is for

Designed for the CBSA Hearings and Litigation Division, but it can be used by other government stakeholders as well.

Why it was made

The public information on immigration cases is scattered over several websites. Here, "how has the Court treated this issue?" takes minutes, not hours.

What it adds

Outcomes by issue, who cites what and how it turned out, the Court's record on every file, and a citation check for your own drafts.

What it cites (left)

Every case and statute in a decision is marked in the text, linked to the exact paragraph or section. Hover to read it, or click to open it beside the one you are reading.

Who cites it (right)

For any decision, the later decisions that cite it, which courts, and how those files ended. That shows whether an authority still carries weight, and for which side.

Why it matters

Every citation is a piece of legal evidence: it shows which authorities a judge found persuasive and which arguments have worked. iLit has read it out of the decisions and stored it, so it can be searched, counted and followed in both directions.

Where files go

The whole docket, not only the decisions

Most Federal Court immigration files never end in a published decision, and leave is usually decided without written reasons. iLit also holds the Court's record of every file on its docket, so it can show the whole picture.

What iLit is not

Not legal advice

iLit is a research aid. It does not advise and it does not predict results.

Not official

A proof of concept, not affiliated with the Government of Canada in any way. No court has endorsed it.

Private for now

The site runs as a private website on a personal computer, using only published, public information. Every decision links to its original.

Not always right

Outcomes and labels come from fixed rules and AI models. Where a figure is uncertain, the page says so.
