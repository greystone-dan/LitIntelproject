MEMORANDUM OF FACT AND LAW (SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT, NOT A REAL FILING)

Applicant: Ms. A. Test
Respondent: The Minister of Citizenship and Immigration

PART I - OVERVIEW

1. This is an application for judicial review of a decision refusing humanitarian and compassionate relief under subsection 25(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, S.C. 2001, c. 27 (IRPA). The application is brought under section 18.1 of the Federal Courts Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. F-7.

2. The decision is reviewed on the reasonableness standard: Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration) v. Vavilov, 2019 SCC 65 at paras 10, 99.

PART II - FACTS

3. The Applicant has lived in Canada for nine years with her two Canadian-born children. She applied under s. 25(1) of the IRPA in March 2023.

4. The officer refused the application, stating that the best interests of the children were "not determinative". The officer did not examine what the children would lose on removal.

PART III - ISSUES

5. Did the officer unreasonably assess the best interests of the child?

6. Did the officer apply the wrong test to hardship?

PART IV - LAW AND ARGUMENT

7. The officer must be "alert, alive and sensitive" to the best interests of affected children: Baker v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), [1999] 2 S.C.R. 817 at para 75.

8. Hardship under s. 25(1) is not limited to "unusual and undeserved" hardship: Kanthasamy v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2015 SCC 61 at paras 21-23, 33.

9. Vavilov, above at para 99, requires that the decision be justified, intelligible and transparent. Ibid at para 100.

10. The deference owed to administrative decision makers is well settled: see Canada (Citizenship and Immigration) v. Khosa, 2009 SCC 12 at paras 59-62; Dunsmuir v. New Brunswick, 2008 SCC 9 at para 47.

11. The Respondent relies on Smith v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2021 FC 999 at para 14. That decision is distinguishable.

12. Section 72(1) of the IRPA requires leave. Subsection 25(1) gives the Minister discretion, but section 3(3)(f) of the IRPA requires that the Act be applied consistently with Canada's human rights obligations.

PART V - ORDER SOUGHT

13. The Applicant asks that the decision be set aside and the matter returned for redetermination by a different officer.
