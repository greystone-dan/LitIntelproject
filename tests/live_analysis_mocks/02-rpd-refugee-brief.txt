BRIEF OF ARGUMENT (SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT, NOT A REAL FILING)

A. Overview

1. The Refugee Protection Division found the Appellant not credible and refused his claim under sections 96 and 97 of the IRPA. The Appellant seeks judicial review.

B. Credibility findings

2. Credibility findings attract deference: Aguebor v. Canada (Minister of Employment and Immigration), [1993] 160 N.R. 315 (F.C.A.). The Court will intervene where findings are not justified in light of the record: Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration) v. Vavilov, 2019 SCC 65 at para 126.

3. The Board did not engage with the documentary evidence, including the arrest warrant. See Cepeda-Gutierrez v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), [1998] F.C.J. No. 1425 at para 17.

C. Section 97 and the Charter

4. Section 97(1)(b) of the IRPA protects a person who faces a risk to life or a risk of cruel and unusual treatment. The Board's analysis of state protection was incomplete.

5. Removal to a risk of torture engages section 7 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms: Suresh v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2002 SCC 1 at para 76. See also Charkaoui v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2007 SCC 9 at para 12.

6. Suresh, supra at para 78, confirms that the balance is contextual. Id.

D. Procedural fairness

7. The Board relied on a country condition document that was not disclosed. Procedural fairness requires disclosure: Baker v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), [1999] 2 SCR 817 at paras 22-28; IRPR s. 170(g) and section 162(2) of the IRPA are to the same effect. The Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations, SOR/2002-227 (IRPR) provide that the Division must give notice of documents.

E. Relief

8. The Appellant asks that the decision be set aside.
